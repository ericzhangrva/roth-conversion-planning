What happens when a non-engineer stops waiting for permission and starts building with agentic AI?

Over the past two weeks, I decided to find out. I didn’t want to use AI just to summarize meeting notes or polish emails. I wanted to see how far a non-technical creator could push modern AI agents to build real, working tools across five wildly different domains:

1. 🎮 **A Nostalgic Web Game:** Recreating a 1990s childhood deductive logic game (*Bulls & Cows*) from scratch and deploying it live to the web in under 90 minutes.
2. 📊 **A 40-Year Quantitative Wealth Model:** Running a 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo simulation to stress-test our family’s asset allocations and long-term tax harvesting strategy before meeting our advisor.
3. 🩺 **A Clinical Health Analytics Dashboard:** Scraping and normalizing years of fragmented hospital lab reports to track my wife’s longitudinal cholesterol and metabolic trends.
4. 📐 **A Deep Dive into Fermat’s Last Theorem:** Distilling Sir Andrew Wiles’ monumental 130-page proof into a transparent 7-page geometric roadmap, and exploring its recent complete autoformalization in Lean 4.
5. 💳 **A Point-of-Sale FinTech App (*OptiRewards*):** Cataloging 168 credit cards across 13 major banks with GPS nearby-store detection and custom point valuation sliders so you never swipe the wrong card at checkout.

Each project started as a simple question or a real personal need. Each ended as working code, interactive tools, or rigorous mathematical synthesis.

Over the coming week, I am going to share the story behind each of these five projects—what was built, how it works, and what it looks like in practice.

Then, on the final day, I will share the unfiltered reality: the real limitations, the subtle traps, and the biggest lessons I learned building with AI as a non-engineer.

Tomorrow, we start with the warm-up: how a 30-year-old childhood memory from an electronic dictionary became a deployed web application during a single lunch break.
